The rapid expansion of urban areas represents one of the most profound spatial transformations of the 21st century, reshaping natural landscapes, ecosystems, and socio-economic structures worldwide. In Nigeria, particularly in the southwestern region, urban growth has intensified due to population increase, rural-urban migration, economic opportunities, and infrastructural development (Onatayo & Ajani, 2022; Adams et al., 2024; Adekunle et al., 2024). While urbanization creates opportunities for economic development and improved access to services, it also poses significant challenges, including environmental degradation, uncontrolled land use change, and pressure on infrastructure (Onanuga et al., 2021; Adegboyega et al., 2024). These challenges highlight the need for effective monitoring, analysis, and prediction of urban growth patterns to support sustainable development planning (Chukwurah et al., 2022; Faiyetole, 2023).

The integration of Geographic Information Systems (GIS) and Remote Sensing technologies has transformed the study of urban growth by enabling efficient acquisition, analysis, and visualization of spatial and temporal data (Gilbert & Shi, 2023; Salam et al., 2023). GIS provides a robust framework for managing and analyzing spatial information, while Remote Sensing allows for the observation of the Earth's surface through satellite imagery without physical contact. Together, these technologies support spatio-temporal analysis, which examines spatial patterns and their evolution over time, providing critical insights for urban planning and environmental management.

#### 2.2.6 Previous Research on Akinyele LGA - ADD NEW SECTION

**Add this new subsection under "Studies on Nigerian Cities and Akinyele LGA/Ibadan":**

### Previous Research on Akinyele LGA

Recent research by Kolawole et al. (2025) specifically focused on Akinyele Local Government Area, examining the Urban Heat Island (UHI) effect using remote sensing and GIS technology. The study analyzed land use changes, temperature variations, and population growth in Akinyele LGA from 2004 to 2024 using Landsat 7 and 8 datasets and ArcGIS Software. Their findings revealed significant land use transformations: urban or built-up areas increased from 14.04% to 23.09%, forest areas increased from 29.78% to 37.82%, while light vegetation reduced from 56.18% to 39.09% over the 20-year period. 

Concurrently, the study documented a rising temperature trend, with mean temperature increasing from 29.574°C in 2004 to 31.583°C in 2024. The population of Akinyele was projected to grow from 211,811 in 2006 to approximately 315,361 by 2024, driven by an annual growth rate of 2.39%. Future projections indicated that built-up areas would reach 119.71 km² by 2034, with land surface temperature (LST) rising to 36.031°C. These findings underscore the accelerating Urban Heat Island effect in Akinyele LGA and highlight the urgent need for effective urban planning strategies to mitigate the impacts of rapid urbanization and rising temperatures (Kolawole et al., 2025).

This research provides critical baseline data for understanding the specific dynamics of urban growth in Akinyele LGA and complements broader studies on Ibadan and Oyo State. The documented patterns of land use change and temperature increase align with similar trends observed across southwestern Nigeria (Onatayo & Ajani, 2022; Salam et al., 2023), while also revealing location-specific characteristics that require targeted intervention strategies.
