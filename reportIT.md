# CHAPTER ONE: INTRODUCTION

## 1.0 Introduction

The field of Surveying and Geoinformatics is a discipline that combines theoretical knowledge with practical application to address real-world challenges in land measurement, mapping, spatial data analysis, and geospatial information management. While academic institutions provide students with fundamental theoretical concepts, principles, and methodologies, the practical application of these concepts in professional settings requires hands-on experience with modern surveying equipment, software applications, and industry-standard procedures.

The Students Industrial Work Experience Scheme (SIWES) serves as a critical bridge between classroom learning and professional practice. It provides students with the opportunity to apply theoretical knowledge in real-life situations, gain exposure to contemporary surveying technologies, understand workplace dynamics, and develop professional competencies essential for successful careers in surveying and geoinformatics. This industrial training experience is designed to complement academic instruction by exposing students to the practical realities of the surveying profession, including fieldwork challenges, client interactions, project management, and adherence to professional ethics and standards.

This report documents the six-month industrial training undertaken at AMAREM SURVEY CONSULT, Minna, Niger State, detailing the organizational structure of the establishment, the scope of training activities, specific work experiences, skills acquired, and recommendations for enhancing the SIWES program.

## 1.1 SIWES Overview

The Students Industrial Work Experience Scheme (SIWES) is an approved skills training program designed to bridge the gap between theoretical knowledge acquired in academic institutions and the practical skills required in professional practice. The scheme exposes students to industry-relevant equipment, modern work methods, safety protocols, and professional standards that are essential for successful transition from academic life to the workplace.

SIWES is a collaborative effort involving multiple stakeholders, including institutions of higher learning, industrial establishments, the Federal Government of Nigeria, the Industrial Training Fund (ITF), and the National Universities Commission (NUC). The program is structured to ensure that students gain meaningful practical experience in their respective fields of study while maintaining academic standards and professional ethics.

For students in the Department of Surveying and Geoinformatics, SIWES provides exposure to various surveying operations, including cadastral surveys, topographic surveys, engineering surveys, hydrographic surveys, geodetic surveys, and geospatial data processing. Students learn to operate modern surveying instruments such as total stations, GPS receivers, levels, theodolites, and drones, while also gaining proficiency in industry-standard software applications for data processing, mapping, and spatial analysis.

The scheme typically spans a period of six months (twenty-four weeks), during which students are attached to approved surveying firms, government agencies, or organizations with surveying departments. Throughout this period, students work under the supervision of experienced professionals who guide them through various projects and operations, ensuring that they acquire relevant skills and competencies.

## 1.2 History of SIWES

The Students Industrial Work Experience Scheme (SIWES) was established in 1973 by the Industrial Training Fund (ITF) in response to a critical gap identified in the Nigerian educational system. Prior to the establishment of SIWES, there was a growing concern among employers and industry stakeholders that graduates from Nigerian tertiary institutions lacked adequate practical skills and hands-on experience necessary for effective performance in the workplace. Graduates possessed strong theoretical knowledge but struggled to apply this knowledge in real-world industrial settings, leading to reduced productivity and the need for extensive on-the-job training by employers.

Recognizing this challenge, the Federal Government of Nigeria, through the Industrial Training Fund, initiated the SIWES program to ensure that students in tertiary institutions receive practical training in industries relevant to their fields of study before graduation. The program was designed to expose students to modern equipment, contemporary work methods, and professional practices that would enhance their employability and reduce the skills gap between academic preparation and industry requirements.

Since its inception, SIWES has evolved to become a mandatory component of academic programs in Nigerian universities, polytechnics, and colleges of education, particularly for science, engineering, technology, and vocational courses. The program has been instrumental in producing graduates who are better prepared for the demands of the workplace and who can contribute meaningfully to national development.

The management and coordination of SIWES involve various government bodies, including the Industrial Training Fund (ITF), which provides funding and oversight; the National Universities Commission (NUC), which ensures compliance in universities; the National Board for Technical Education (NBTE), which oversees polytechnics; and the National Commission for Colleges of Education (NCCE), which supervises colleges of education. These bodies work collaboratively with academic institutions and industrial establishments to ensure the effective implementation of the program.

## 1.3 Objectives of SIWES

According to the Industrial Training Fund's Policy Document No. 1 of 1973, the Students Industrial Work Experience Scheme was established with the following specific objectives:

1. **Acquiring Industrial Skills and Experience**: To provide students with the opportunity to acquire practical skills and hands-on experience in their fields of study by working in industrial and professional environments. This exposure enables students to understand how theoretical concepts are applied in real-world situations.

2. **Preparing for Work Situations**: To prepare students for the work environment they will encounter after graduation by exposing them to workplace culture, professional ethics, organizational structures, and the dynamics of working in teams. This preparation reduces the transition shock that many graduates experience when entering the workforce.

3. **Exposure to Modern Equipment and Technology**: To expose students to current equipment, machinery, techniques, and technologies used in industry. This exposure is particularly important in fields like surveying and geoinformatics, where technology evolves rapidly, and students need to be familiar with contemporary tools and software applications.

4. **Easing Transition to the Workforce**: To make the transition from academic life to professional employment smoother by providing students with practical experience, professional networks, and an understanding of workplace expectations. Students who complete SIWES are better positioned to secure employment and perform effectively in their roles.

5. **Applying Academic Knowledge**: To provide students with opportunities to apply the theoretical knowledge acquired in the classroom to practical problems and projects. This application reinforces learning, deepens understanding, and helps students appreciate the relevance of their academic studies.

6. **Strengthening Employer Involvement in Education**: To enrich the educational process by involving employers and industry professionals in the training of students. This involvement ensures that academic curricula remain relevant to industry needs and that students are trained according to current professional standards.

7. **Developing Professional Competencies**: To help students develop essential professional competencies such as problem-solving, critical thinking, communication, teamwork, time management, and ethical conduct. These competencies are crucial for success in any professional career.

8. **Fostering Self-Reliance and Entrepreneurship**: To encourage students to develop self-reliance, confidence, and entrepreneurial skills by exposing them to how businesses and organizations operate. This exposure can inspire students to establish their own enterprises after graduation.

## 1.4 Bodies Involved in the Management of SIWES Programme

The successful implementation of the Students Industrial Work Experience Scheme requires the collaboration of multiple stakeholders, each playing specific roles in the management, coordination, funding, and supervision of the program. The key bodies involved include:

### 1. Federal Government of Nigeria
The Federal Government provides policy direction, regulatory framework, and financial support for the SIWES program. Through various ministries and agencies, the government ensures that the program aligns with national educational policies and contributes to the development of a skilled workforce for national development.

### 2. Industrial Training Fund (ITF)
The Industrial Training Fund is the primary coordinating body for SIWES. Established by Decree No. 47 of 1971, the ITF is responsible for:
- Funding the SIWES program through allowances paid to students during their industrial attachment
- Coordinating the placement of students in approved industrial establishments
- Monitoring and evaluating the implementation of the program
- Ensuring compliance with SIWES guidelines and standards
- Maintaining a database of approved establishments for student placement
- Organizing orientation programs for students and supervisors

### 3. National Universities Commission (NUC)
The National Universities Commission oversees the implementation of SIWES in Nigerian universities. The NUC ensures that:
- Universities comply with SIWES requirements and guidelines
- Academic programs incorporate SIWES as a mandatory component
- Institutions maintain quality standards in the supervision and assessment of students
- SIWES credits are appropriately integrated into academic curricula

### 4. National Board for Technical Education (NBTE)
The NBTE supervises the implementation of SIWES in polytechnics and similar institutions. The board ensures that:
- Polytechnics adhere to SIWES guidelines and standards
- Students in technical and vocational programs receive appropriate industrial training
- The program meets the practical training requirements for technical education

### 5. National Commission for Colleges of Education (NCCE)
The NCCE oversees SIWES implementation in colleges of education, ensuring that:
- Teacher training programs include practical industrial experience where applicable
- Students are adequately prepared for teaching careers through exposure to educational institutions and relevant industries

### 6. Institutions of Higher Learning
Universities, polytechnics, and colleges of education are responsible for:
- Identifying and vetting suitable industrial establishments for student placement
- Appointing institution-based supervisors to monitor students during their attachment
- Preparing students for industrial training through pre-SIWES orientation
- Assessing students' performance and awarding appropriate credits
- Maintaining communication with industrial establishments and the ITF
- Ensuring students comply with SIWES rules and regulations

### 7. Employers of Labour (Industrial Establishments)
Surveying firms, government agencies, construction companies, and other organizations that accept students for industrial training are responsible for:
- Providing meaningful training experiences aligned with students' fields of study
- Assigning experienced professionals as industrial-based supervisors
- Exposing students to relevant equipment, technologies, and work methods
- Ensuring students' safety and welfare during the attachment period
- Providing feedback on students' performance through assessment reports
- Complying with SIWES guidelines and standards

### 8. Students
Students participating in SIWES have the responsibility to:
- Comply with the rules and regulations of both their institutions and the industrial establishments
- Demonstrate professionalism, punctuality, and commitment to learning
- Maintain proper documentation of their training activities
- Submit required reports and assessments to their institutions
- Respect confidentiality and professional ethics in handling organizational information

The collaborative efforts of these stakeholders ensure that SIWES achieves its objectives of bridging the gap between academic theory and industrial practice, thereby producing graduates who are well-prepared for professional careers.

### 2.3.3 Data Processing and Plotting

Data processing and plotting are critical components of modern surveying practice. AMAREM SURVEY CONSULT employs advanced software and techniques for processing survey data and creating visual representations of survey results.

**a) Data Processing**

Data processing involves transforming raw survey measurements into usable information. The key activities include:

- **Data Cleaning**: Removing errors, outliers, and inconsistencies from raw survey data to ensure accuracy and reliability.

- **Coordinate Transformation**: Converting survey measurements from local coordinate systems to national or international coordinate systems (e.g., UTM, WGS84) to ensure compatibility with other spatial data.

- **Calculating Elevations**: Computing elevations of survey points relative to established vertical datums, including orthometric heights and ellipsoidal heights.

- **Adjusting for Distortions**: Applying corrections for atmospheric conditions, earth curvature, instrument errors, and other factors that may affect survey measurements.

- **Survey Adjustment**: Performing least squares adjustment and other mathematical techniques to distribute measurement errors and optimize the accuracy of survey networks.

**b) Plotting (Creating Visual Representations)**

Plotting involves creating maps, plans, and other visual representations of survey data. AMAREM SURVEY CONSULT produces:

- **Topographic Maps**: Detailed maps showing terrain features, elevations, contours, and natural and man-made features.

- **Plan Drawings**: Cadastral plans, site plans, layout plans, and other drawings that depict land boundaries, dimensions, and features.

- **Cross-Sections and Profiles**: Vertical sections showing elevation changes along specific lines, commonly used in road design and drainage planning.

- **3D Models**: Three-dimensional representations of terrain and structures, useful for visualization, analysis, and design purposes.

- **Graphs and Charts**: Visual representations of survey data for analysis and presentation purposes.

**Tools Used**

AMAREM SURVEY CONSULT utilizes industry-standard software and tools for data processing and plotting, including:

- **CAD Software**: AutoCAD, Civil 3D, and MicroStation for creating detailed drawings, plans, and designs.

## 3.3 Boundary Demarcation and Establishment

Boundary demarcation and establishment of land parcels constitute critical aspects of cadastral surveying. I gained extensive practical experience in these activities during my attachment at AMAREM SURVEY CONSULT.

**a) Boundary Demarcation**

Boundary demarcation involves determining and marking the exact boundaries of land parcels on the ground. This process requires:

- **Survey Planning**: Reviewing existing survey records, title documents, and previous survey plans to understand the legal boundaries of the property.

- **Field Reconnaissance**: Conducting site visits to identify existing boundary markers, physical features, and potential challenges before commencing the survey.

- **Instrument Setup**: Setting up surveying instruments such as total stations and GPS receivers at appropriate control points or reference stations.

- **Boundary Measurement**: Using total stations, GPS receivers, and traditional measuring equipment to accurately measure and locate boundary corners based on legal descriptions and survey records.

- **Verification**: Verifying the accuracy of boundary measurements by checking distances, angles, and closures, and comparing with existing survey records.

The establishment of land parcels involves conducting surveys to determine the precise location, dimensions, and features of properties. This includes:

- **Control Survey**: Establishing or connecting to existing survey control points to provide a reference framework for the survey.

- **Determining Dimensions**: Computing the lengths of boundary lines, angles between boundaries, and the total area of the land parcel.

- **Interpreting Plans**: Reading and interpreting survey plans, legal descriptions, and title documents to understand the intended boundaries and features of the property.

- **Establishing Reference Points**: Setting up permanent reference points or benchmarks that can be used for future surveys or construction activities on the property.

## 4.1 Recommendations

Based on my six-month industrial training experience at AMAREM SURVEY CONSULT, I offer the following recommendations to enhance the effectiveness of the SIWES program for future participants:

### 4.1.1 Recommendations for the University

1. **Fair Treatment of Students**: The university should ensure that all students are treated fairly and equitably in the SIWES placement process, with transparent criteria for assigning students to industrial establishments. Students should be placed in organizations that can provide meaningful training experiences relevant to their field of study.

2. **Timely Submission of Student Lists**: The university should submit student lists and placement information to the Industrial Training Fund (ITF) and industrial establishments in a timely manner to avoid delays in student placement and allowance payments. Early communication facilitates better planning by both students and host organizations.

3. **Serious Supervision Protocols**: The university should implement more rigorous supervision protocols, including regular visits by institution-based supervisors to industrial establishments to monitor student progress, address challenges, and ensure that students are receiving appropriate training. Supervisors should provide constructive feedback and support to students throughout the attachment period.

4. **Pre-SIWES Orientation**: The university should conduct comprehensive pre-SIWES orientation programs that prepare students for industrial training, including information on workplace expectations, professional conduct, safety protocols, report writing, and how to maximize learning opportunities during the attachment.

5. **Industry Partnerships**: The university should strengthen partnerships with surveying firms and relevant organizations to ensure a sufficient number of quality placement opportunities for students. Regular engagement with industry partners can help align academic curricula with industry needs.

### 4.1.2 Recommendations for the Establishment

1. **Provision of Accommodation**: Industrial establishments, particularly those located far from students' institutions, should consider providing accommodation or accommodation allowances to students to reduce the financial burden of securing housing during the attachment period. This would enable students to focus more on their training rather than accommodation challenges.

2. **Minimum Stipends for Students**: While the ITF provides allowances to students, these are often insufficient to cover living expenses, especially in urban areas. Establishments should consider providing additional stipends or allowances to students to support their welfare during the training period.

3. **Reduced Work Pressure**: While it is important for students to experience realistic workplace conditions, establishments should be mindful of the learning objectives of SIWES and avoid placing excessive work pressure on students. Students should have adequate time for learning, reflection, and documentation of their experiences.

4. **Appropriate Training Opportunities**: Establishments should ensure that students are exposed to a variety of surveying operations and are given opportunities to participate actively in projects rather than being assigned only to routine or menial tasks. A structured training program with clear learning objectives would enhance the quality of the training experience.

5. **Mentorship and Guidance**: Establishments should assign experienced professionals as mentors to guide students throughout their attachment, providing regular feedback, answering questions, and helping students develop professional competencies.

6. **Safety Training and Equipment**: Establishments should provide comprehensive safety training to students and ensure that they are equipped with appropriate personal protective equipment (PPE) for field operations.

### 4.1.3 Recommendations for the Industrial Training Fund (ITF)

1. **Actions Against Non-Compliant Organizations**: The ITF should take stronger actions against organizations that accept students for industrial training but fail to provide meaningful training experiences or comply with SIWES guidelines. This includes organizations that use students primarily for menial tasks or fail to assign qualified supervisors.

2. **Monitoring and Evaluation Measures**: The ITF should implement more robust monitoring and evaluation mechanisms to assess the quality of training provided by industrial establishments. This could include regular inspections, student feedback surveys, and performance assessments of host organizations.

3. **Timely Payment of Allowances**: The ITF should ensure that student allowances are paid promptly and consistently throughout the attachment period. Delays in allowance payments create financial hardship for students and undermine the effectiveness of the program.

4. **Increased Allowance Amounts**: The ITF should consider increasing the allowance amounts to reflect current economic realities and the cost of living. The current allowances are often insufficient to cover students' basic needs during the attachment period.

5. **Expansion of Approved Establishments**: The ITF should work to expand the database of approved establishments for student placement, particularly in emerging areas of surveying and geoinformatics such as drone surveying, LiDAR technology, and advanced GIS applications.

6. **Feedback Mechanisms**: The ITF should establish effective feedback mechanisms that allow students to report challenges, provide input on their training experiences, and suggest improvements to the SIWES program.
